\begin{abstract}
\noindent We study the Sequential Matching Hackathon: twice daily for 60 days
(+40 follow-up), a policy spends a 12-unit ask budget and proposes disjoint
feasible pairs to maximise mutual second-meeting intentions (MSMI) per 100
arrived members, averaged over six scenario families. Our contribution is
measurement-first. Only \textbf{1.24\%} of pairs are hard-feasible (0.24\%
sparse); one third of members can never be matched (a declined hard field is
permanent); \textbf{98.5\%} of every introduction is lost to mechanisms no
policy controls, above all a 3-day answer window that discards $\sim$64\% of
otherwise-successful outcomes. The only reliable pair signal is agreement on
\textbf{4 of 7} soft fields. The reachable edge set caps introductions
($|E|\approx103$ development, $\approx$23 sparse, $\approx$91 cold start) and
the greedy baseline already realises \textbf{89--99\%} of the corrected
ceiling on identical seeds --- so this is an \textbf{acquisition problem, not
an RL problem}, with honest headroom $\approx$1.3--1.6$\times$. Our implemented
stdlib-only policy (matchability-gated VOI acquisition, exact general-graph
maximum-weight matching, single-pass anchored online weights) climbs a paired
ablation ladder 0.333 $\to$ \textbf{0.514} (directional: the headline is
underpowered by $\sim$20$\times$, mechanism metrics corroborate). Notably the
fixed-prior variant beats the adaptive one everywhere on our seeds --- exactly
as the noise analysis predicts. All numbers come from 13 stdlib-only scripts
against the public simulator; every person and outcome is synthetic.
\end{abstract}

\section{Problem interpretation}
Each of 60 decision days the policy (i) issues clarification asks costing
$\le 12$ units (hard-constraint bundle $=3$, one named soft field $=1$,
immediate, exact-or-declined; declined is permanent), then (ii) proposes a
batch of pairwise-disjoint feasible pairs (empty = wait). Every pair must pass
reciprocal hard checks both directions (age ranges, genders, zones, smoking,
children, relationship structure, schedule overlap), be previously unintroduced,
and have all hard fields known. Feedback (directional yes/no, date, second
yes) matures with delay; after day 60, 40 follow-up days resolve outcomes with
no new actions. MSMI requires both yes $\to$ date within 30 days $\to$ both
second-yes within 3 days of the date. Primary score: mean over six families
(standard, sparse, cold start, delayed, shifted weights, drift) $\times$ 20
private seeds. Tiebreakers: coverage, mutual acceptances, lower ask cost,
lower inference time. Withheld: seeds, arrivals, hidden preferences, reply
rates. Execution: JSON in/out $\le 1$\,MiB, 10\,s/call, 2 cores, 1\,GiB, CPU,
offline; memory self-carried.

Three facts reframe the challenge (verified in \texttt{soft\_fields.py}):
(a)~soft values are \emph{static} (0/200 changed over 60 days) --- asking is a
lossless one-time purchase; drift shifts the \emph{scoring formula} ($-0.5$
logit from day 35), not preferences. (b)~Only 11.4\% of pairs (3.8\% cold
start) have complete fit before asking vs 56\% after buying the four fields.
(c)~Only \texttt{relationship\_goal}, \texttt{relationship\_pace},
\texttt{lifestyle}, \texttt{conversations} enter the scorer (weights
0.70/0.40/0.25/0.20; shifted: 0.25/0.80/\textbf{$-$}0.25/0.50);
\texttt{emotional\_availability}, \texttt{space\_for\_relationship},
\texttt{relocate} are dead weight (force-changing them leaves MSMI
bit-identical). Never ask about those three.

\section{Hypothesis}
A reciprocal pair scorer learned online from delayed/censored feedback, paired
with global maximum-weight allocation and VOI-ranked clarification, raises
MSMI/100 over greedy via (i)~learned pair weights vs uniform soft-count,
(ii)~global allocation vs greedy fill, (iii)~budgeted soft acquisition vs
none, (iv)~drift-aware sequencing. Full RL is rejected: Learn2Match's
friction-loss result predicts failure exactly when volume is saturated and the
gap is friction. External LLM scoring is rejected (unavailable offline;
conversations are authored templates).

\section{Reciprocal feasibility: introductions are capped}
First-failing-rule attribution on truth (20 seeds): \texttt{who\_to\_meet}
57\%, age 26\%, geography 12\% (19\% sparse) --- the rest $\sim$4\% combined.

$P(\text{matchable})=0.667/0.656/0.600$: one third can never be matched.
$|E|$ is a hard ceiling (no repeats). An apparent sparse contradiction
($|E|=20.9$ vs 22.6 realised) was a seed-set mismatch: on identical seeds
$|E|=22.8$ vs 22.6 realised, and assigned $\subseteq$ reachable held with 0
violations. Clarification is \textbf{supply-limited, not budget-limited}:
720 units vs $\sim$80 askable members, exhausted by $\sim$day 20. Sparse is
therefore saturated, not an opportunity; headroom is cold-start volume plus
shift/drift ordering.

\section{Probability analysis (ranking only)}
Recovered law per assigned pair: one shared shock
$s\sim\mathcal{N}(0,0.45^2)$, then per direction
$\text{accept}\sim\text{Bern}(\sigma(-0.25+b_a+\mathrm{fit}+s+\mathrm{drift}))$,
$\text{respond}\sim\text{Bern}(r_a)$; date w.p.\ 0.78 at
$t+\max(\text{delays})+U\{1..14\}$; second yes
$\sigma(0.15+sb_a+s+0.4[\text{goal match}])$ answered w.p.\ $r_a$, delay
$U\{1..5\}$. Directions correlate ($\rho\approx0.43$ via fit $+$ shared): never
multiply marginals; integrate over $s$ (41-node rule, error $<5{\times}10^{-6}$).
Single-pair replay validates ranking (top decile 0.041 pred / 0.033 emp vs
bottom 0.002/0.000). Funnel (greedy, development): 91.6 intros $\to$ 52.0 both
respond (57\%) $\to$ 12.6 mutual (24\%, \textbf{the controllable stage}) $\to$
10.3 dates $\to$ 3.7 both second-yes $\to$ 1.3 in-window $\to$ \textbf{$\sim$1.0
MSMI}. The 3-day window ($(3/5)^2$) alone destroys $\sim$64\%. A ``0.56 vs
1.0'' under-prediction in our draft was a mislabelled single-batch sum; the
corrected joint form (one expectation over $s$ of all four sigmoids, both
response stages, \texttt{p\_msmi\_joint}) gives $\sum\approx1.07$--1.15 vs
$\sim$1.0 realised --- greedy sits at \textbf{87--93\%} of the absolute
ceiling. Rankings are identical under either form. Value spread: top-half vs
bottom-half realised lift 1.38$\times$ (fit) $\to$ 1.48$\times$ (+oracle
response) --- member latents (1--2 obs/member) are not worth learning.
Black-box weight search is ruled out by measurement: exploitable range 0.132
$<$ single-run noise 0.356; a sign-flipped vector scores within noise
(0.410 vs 0.424). Common random numbers cut SE 1.7$\times$ and are mandatory.

\section{Proposed policy (implemented, stdlib-only)}
\textbf{(a) Acquisition, matchability-gated.} v1 spent 183/384 soft asks
(47.7\%) on permanently unmatchable members (replicated) --- now gated out.
Zone-clustered hard clearance first
($2\times$ cleared same-zone peers $+$ uncleared same-zone peers); leftover on
the 4 live soft fields in tiers: T0 (in a currently feasible pair) $\to$ T1
(cleared pool) $\to$ T2 (uncleared matchable), by $|w_k|$ with partner-observed
preference. Time-adaptive bar (free at day 0, $n_{\text{friends}}\ge2$ by day 20).
\textbf{(b) Value model.} Observed both sides: $+w_k$ if equal else $-w_k/2$;
unknown: $\mathbb{E}=w_k(1.5/|S_k|-0.5)$ under the uniform prior (exact here;
kills the \texttt{None}-equals-\texttt{None} artefact). \textbf{(c) Weights.}
Single-pass streaming logistic on matured directional responses (v1 re-fed
cumulative feedback, updating day-5 events $\sim$55$\times$ --- fixed via
\texttt{seen\_events}); $\eta=0.25/\sqrt{1+n}$, anchor $\lambda=0.10$ to the
development prior, clip 1.0. Synthetic validation: clean data recovers pace
0.83 vs true 0.80; small-$n$ old-style updates sign-flip, the fix does not.
\textbf{(d) Allocation.} Exact general-graph MWM (branch-and-bound over
components, greedy warm start; graph is non-bipartite, $\sim$47\% same-gender
acceptance): \textbf{268/268 fuzz-optimal} vs brute force, $<1$\,ms/day. A
$-0.10$ post-day-35 offset is a declared fixed parameter, not a drift
detector (Round 2: trigger from observed acceptance drop).

\section{Missing and delayed data}
Declined $\ne$ not-asked (permanent, never bought). Missing responses are
censored, never imputed as No; only matured outcomes enter updates; pending
queue keyed on expected observation day. Assignment-day feature snapshots (no
leakage; the updater only changes future weights from matured outcomes, and
static fields remove staleness). No IPS claims (\texttt{propensity} is null;
pool-level splits only; controlled same-seed simulator comparisons).
Memory $\sim$10\,KB (Beta counters, 12-dim posterior, pending queue).
Exploration via Thompson sampling over pool priors; rank-only decisions, no
hard cutoff; rolling posterior-predictive coverage check doubles as the
wrong-belief detector.

\section{Evaluation}
Baselines (identical seeds, all six families): greedy, no-clarification,
random-feasible. Ablations: no soft acquisition; fixed vs online weights
(isolates shift); greedy-fill vs MWM; no drift scheduling; member bandit off;
$\theta$ sweep. Measured ladder (seeds 101--106, paired):

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
Configuration & MSMI/100 & vs greedy & Mutual acc. \\
\midrule
Organiser greedy & 0.333 & --- & 10.14 \\
Candidate v1 & 0.347 & +4.2\% & 10.56 \\
VOI ask + greedy match & 0.389 & +16.7\% & 10.50 \\
Full (VOI + MWM + adaptive) & 0.444 & +33.3\% & 10.81 \\
Full (VOI + MWM + fixed prior) & \textbf{0.514} & \textbf{+54.2\%} & \textbf{11.20} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Honest reading: at $n=36$ the minimum detectable effect is $\sim$0.175, so
only the fixed-prior headline reaches it and per-variant cells ($n=6$) are
illustrative. Corroboration: monotone ladder, +10\% mutual acceptances, and
fixed-prior beating adaptive \emph{everywhere including shift} (0.500 vs
0.417) --- exactly as the noise analysis predicts ($\sim$50 Bernoulli trials
cannot carry 4 weights). The adaptive arm stays for Round 2, tighter anchored:
it is the only mechanism handling shift without variant labels. Cost note: we
spend $\sim$409 vs greedy's $\sim$198 ask units, losing the cost tiebreaker
(Round 2: cost-capped ablation). Harness smoke test (official
\texttt{evaluate.py}, seed 101, development): greedy msmi=1 $\to$ team msmi=3,
both valid, 0.15\,s/call.

\section{Failure cases and limitations}
Withheld answers ($\sim$1/3 permanently unmatchable, kept in denominators);
saturated sparse supply; 8-day-busy schedule coupling (why global matching);
right-censoring (day-55 intros resolve $\sim$day 77, unseen by the policy);
adaptation variance (above). All outcomes are synthetic; nothing here is
evidence about real relationships. Kit vendored verbatim (upstream
\texttt{RomeoJulietLove/The-Sequential-Matching-Problem}, pin
\texttt{a8e26b3}, MIT + synthetic-data licence); our work is in
\texttt{research/}; teammate Oviya's branch merged with history preserved and
policy files relocated out of the kit subtree. Reproduce:
\texttt{python3 -m unittest -v} (22 OK) + \texttt{verify\_data.py}, then
\texttt{research/analysis/\{prob\_model,validate\_model,why\_cap,ceiling,value\_spread,candidate,verify\_team\_policy,team\_policy\_eval\}.py}.
